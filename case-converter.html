Why it matters

Why consistent casing matters more than it looks

Casing is part of your brand and your professionalism. A headline with inconsistent capitalisation looks careless, and a filename with mixed separators breaks conventions that other people rely on. Applying one rule to the whole document removes that inconsistency in seconds.

Developers depend on naming conventions. Code that mixes snake_case, camelCase and PascalCase in the same file is harder to read and harder to search. Converting a block of identifiers to the convention your project uses is a routine task that a converter handles instantly.

Casing also affects how text behaves downstream. A URL slug with spaces or capitals may break or duplicate; a kebab-case slug is safe and readable. A meta description in sentence case reads more naturally than one in title case. Choosing the right form is a small but real quality decision.

FAQ

Case converter questions, answered

What is the difference between title case and sentence case?

Title case capitalises the first letter of most words, which suits headlines and titles. Sentence case capitalises only the first word and proper nouns, which reads more naturally in body text and descriptions.

What are snake_case and kebab-case?

Snake case joins words with underscores, as in my_variable_name, and is common in Python and databases. Kebab case joins words with hyphens, as in my-page-slug, and is standard for URLs and CSS class names.

When should I use camelCase or PascalCase?

Camel case capitalises every word after the first, as in myVariableName, and is common in JavaScript. Pascal case capitalises every word including the first, as in MyClassName, and is used for classes and types.
