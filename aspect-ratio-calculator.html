The aspect ratio handbook

Aspect ratio calculator for images, video and social media

An aspect ratio describes the proportional relationship between the width and the height of a frame. It is written as two numbers separated by a colon, such as 16:9, and it stays the same no matter how large the frame becomes. A 1920×1080 monitor, a 3840×2160 television and a 3840×1600 ultrawide panel can all share the 16:9 family, because the ratio survives every change of scale.

This free aspect ratio calculator solves the problem designers, editors and marketers hit every day: you know the ratio you need and one dimension, and you must work out the other. Type the original width and height, enter the new width, and the tool returns the matching height in real time. Flip the inputs to solve for width instead, simplify any pair of numbers to its smallest whole-number form, and preview the resulting proportion against common presets.

Everything runs inside your browser using plain JavaScript arithmetic. There is no upload, no account and no server round-trip, so a client brief, an unreleased product shot or a confidential storyboard never leaves your device.

Why it matters

Why getting the ratio right decides whether your media fits

Cropping and letterboxing are expensive mistakes. Upload a 4:3 asset to a platform that renders 9:16 and the app will either crop away your subject or add ugly black bars. Publish a 16:9 thumbnail where the grid expects 1:1 and your artwork is clipped. Calculating the correct dimensions before you export removes the guesswork and keeps every placement clean.

The maths is simple, but it is easy to get wrong under time pressure. The rule is that the ratio of the original dimensions must equal the ratio of the new dimensions: width one divided by height one equals width two divided by height two. When you know three of those four numbers, the fourth falls out immediately. Our calculator performs that cross-multiplication for you and rounds to whole pixels, because fractional pixels blur edges.

Social platforms add a second layer of complexity, because each one publishes its own preferred ratios. Instagram favours 4:5 for feed posts and 9:16 for Reels. YouTube uses 16:9 for long-form video and 9:16 for Shorts. LinkedIn prefers 1.91:1 for link cards. A calculator that also lists these presets turns a fiddly chore into a two-second check.

FAQ

Aspect ratio questions, answered

What is an aspect ratio?

An aspect ratio is the proportional relationship between width and height, written as two numbers separated by a colon, such as 16:9. It describes the shape of a frame rather than its size, so every frame in the same family keeps that shape at any resolution.

How does the calculator find the missing dimension?

It uses the cross-multiplication rule: width one divided by height one equals width two divided by height two. Supply any three values and it computes the fourth, then rounds to the nearest whole pixel so edges stay crisp.

What is the most common aspect ratio?

16:9 is the default for monitors, televisions and long-form video. 9:16 is its vertical twin for phones and Shorts. 4:5 suits Instagram feed posts, 1:1 is the classic square, and 21:9 is the cinema-style ultrawide.

Can I simplify a ratio like 1920×1080?

Yes. Paste any width and height and the tool divides both numbers by their greatest common divisor, returning the smallest whole-number form. 1920 by 1080 simplifies to 16:9.

Does it work for video as well as images?

It works for any rectangular frame. The arithmetic is identical whether you are sizing a photograph, a video frame, a canvas or a print layout.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser with plain JavaScript. Your dimensions, filenames and presets are never transmitted, stored or logged, and the tool keeps working with your network switched off.

Is the tool free?

Yes. It is completely free, requires no account and carries no ads, pop-ups or tracking pixels.

Does it support print and DPI dimensions?

Yes. Because the ratio is independent of units, you can enter millimetres or inches and the result keeps the same proportion, ready for print specifications.
