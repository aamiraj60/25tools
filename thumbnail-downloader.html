No — and no tool can, because YouTube does not publish thumbnails above 1280 × 720. We tested every documented endpoint (maxresdefault, hq720, maxres2, maxres3 and the numbered variants) and the ceiling is always 1280 × 720. The video itself may be 4K, but the thumbnail still is not. Any site claiming a 4K thumbnail is upscaling a 1280 × 720 image.

YouTube only generates the maxresdefault still when the uploader supplied a large enough source. Older or low-resolution uploads may only have SD and below. When that happens the card is dimmed with a note, and Full HD (1280 × 720) remains the best image available.

Up to six: hq720 and maxresdefault at 1280 × 720, sddefault at 640 × 480, hqdefault at 480 × 360, mqdefault at 320 × 180 and default at 120 × 90. The 480 × 360 image exists for essentially every public video.

Both show the same picture. YouTube's WebP versions are usually a little smaller for the same dimensions, which makes them handy when the thumbnail is going on a website and page weight matters. JPG is the safer choice if the image will be opened in older software.
